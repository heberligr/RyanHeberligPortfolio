Overview

The low-pressure molding station experienced frequent hardware failures and required regular maintenance. Heating cartridges, thermocouples, electrical connections, and coolant lines were prone to damage, while the existing free-hanging wiring made troubleshooting and repairs difficult. These issues limited the station's reliability and restricted the number of molding cycles that could be completed consecutively.

I redesigned the station's power distribution system around a raceway-style layout, replacing the existing free-hanging wiring with an organized and protected system. The new design improved accessibility to electrical components while reducing the likelihood of damage during operation and maintenance. I also replaced the PVC coolant system they were using previously with manifolds to decrease coolant leakage.

I selected and integrated off-the-shelf components with custom-designed parts to improve the durability and functionality of the station. This included reaching out to suppliers for custom heating cartridges with stainless-steel braid protection, upgrading to more robust thermocouples, and replacing incorrectly specced electrical plugs with connectors better matched to the station's voltage requirements. I also coordinated with station operators, making sure that my design decisions made daily operation as easy as possible. Alongside these changes, I 3D printed custom brackets for the raceway and manifolds, manually machined components, and wired and assembled the raceway myself.
