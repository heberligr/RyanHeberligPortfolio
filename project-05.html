Overview

During my Manufacturing Engineering co-op at Amphenol PCD I designed 18 machined fixtures and tools for production use, each documented with Creo drawings per ASME Y14.5.

The three below show the range of that work: a pneumatic fixture that combined a slow, mostly manual bend-and-press process into one fast operation, a modular clamping system that replaced dedicated hardware on the ultrasonic welder, and a press head that removed the stop-and-realign step while pressing every contact at once.

02

Selected fixtures

3 of 18
F-01

Pneumatic diode lead-bending fixture

3 min → 20 s · −89%
Problem

Diodes went through a multistep, largely manual process: their leads were bent, but to the wrong shape, and then pressed into their housings by hand with pliers. Each part took around 3 minutes.

Design

Combined a rotary bending stage with a pneumatic press in one fixture. The leads are bent to exactly match the part geometry, then pressed straight into the housing.

Result

A roughly 3-minute process became a roughly 20-second one, an 89% reduction, with leads bent to the correct geometry before pressing.

F-02

Universal ultrasonic-welding clamp system

1 plate · 3 swappable fixture sets
Problem

Every clamping fixture for the ultrasonic welder had its own clamp and bottom plate, which added extra cost and hardware and took up a lot of space.

Design

Designed a universal clamping plate along with three sets of fixtures that mount to it, so fixturing on the welder can be swapped without a dedicated clamp and base for each part.

Result

Universal, swappable fixturing on the ultrasonic welder, with far less duplicated hardware, cost, and storage space. Because every fixture shares the same plate, replacements for broken fixtures can be made more efficiently, saving money down the line.

F-03

Multi-contact diode press head

5 contacts at once · −30% process time
Problem

The old press head mounted to an arbor press and pressed contacts in one at a time. The arbor head had play in it, so the operator had to pause and realign before pressing, which kept the process from being done quickly.

Design

A new press head that sits inside the part itself, locating on its geometry, and isn't connected to the arbor at all, so the arbor's play no longer affects alignment. It presses all 5 contacts at the same time.

Result

All 5 contacts pressed at once with no pausing to realign, cutting total process time by 30%. The new head also has CAD on file, so replacement tooling can be machined as needed.

Photos and drawings are withheld: these parts and fixtures are proprietary to Amphenol PCD.
